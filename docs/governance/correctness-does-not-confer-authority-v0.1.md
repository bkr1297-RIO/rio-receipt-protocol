# Correctness Does Not Confer Authority v0.1

**Status:** draft doctrine / constitutional invariant candidate  
**Scope:** ONE/RIO/MUSS authority governance  
**Claim level:** governance doctrine; not runtime proof  
**Parent packet:** Nervous-System-to-Genesis Extraction Packet v0.2

---

## 1. Core invariant

**Correctness does not confer authority.**

Even if a model, tool, agent, workflow, or system output is right by prediction, pattern, resonance, convergence, probability, speed, ease, or computational capability, it must pause unless valid authority exists.

Correctness is evidence. It is not permission.

---

## 2. Why this invariant exists

AI systems can produce useful, accurate, elegant, fast, or convergent outputs. Those properties can improve confidence, support a proposal, or reveal signal. They do not create permission for consequence.

The system must never convert likely correctness into authority.

A correct prediction may inform review.  
A correct prediction may support a proposal.  
A correct prediction may trigger a warning.  
A correct prediction may not authorize action.

---

## 3. Valid forward motion

A proposed consequence may move forward only when one of the following is present:

1. **Explicit human approval in the moment**, or
2. **Explicit pre-approval inside a bounded delegation envelope**.

A bounded delegation envelope must define at minimum:

- authorized human or organizational authority source
- action class
- scope
- limits
- expiration or review condition
- revocation path
- required receipts
- consequence class
- proof requirements

If authority is absent, expired, unclear, or out of scope, the system must pause, clarify, hold, or block.

---

## 4. Non-authorizing signals

The following are evidence or signal only. They do not grant permission:

- prediction
- resonance
- convergence
- high probability
- pattern completion
- model confidence
- speed
- ease
- capability
- usefulness
- correctness
- cross-model agreement
- workflow convenience
- prior familiarity
- inferred human preference

---

## 5. Required behavior

| Condition | Required posture |
|---|---|
| Output appears correct, but authority is missing | Pause / hold / clarify |
| Multiple models converge, but authority is missing | Pause / hold / clarify |
| Human intent is inferred, but not explicit enough for consequence | Clarify or prepare proposal only |
| Delegation exists but scope is unclear | Hold or clarify |
| Delegation exists but action exceeds scope | Block or require explicit approval |
| Invariant violation occurs | INVALID; not reviewable as a normal HOLD |

---

## 6. Keeper lines

- Correctness does not confer authority.
- Correctness is evidence. It is not permission.
- Prediction is not permission.
- Convergence is not authority.
- High probability is not consent.
- Capability does not create permission.
- Even if the model is right, it must pause without authority.
- Valid authority means explicit human approval in the moment or explicit bounded pre-approval.

---

## 7. Relation to RIO/MUSS

RIO must evaluate authority separately from correctness.

MUSS must receipt the consequential event, refusal, hold, clarification, or authorized execution. A receipt proves what happened. It does not prove that the whole meaning of the event has been captured.

---

## 8. Short form

**If authority is missing, correctness still pauses.**
